10-Slide Carousel Script

Slide 1 (Hook): Ask someone who they are and, sooner or later, they tell you a story — not a list of traits. You are the narrator of your own life. And the KIND of story you tell shapes your wellbeing. 📖
Slide 2: Psychologists call it NARRATIVE IDENTITY — the internalized story that connects your remembered past to your imagined future and answers: "How did I become me, and where am I going?"
Slide 3: Dr. Dan McAdams (Northwestern) says a full person has 3 layers: traits (your "weather"), goals & values, and your life story. The story is the layer that makes you feel like YOU — and the one we never examine on purpose.
Slide 4: Researchers read a life like a book: Chapters → Key scenes (high point, low point, turning point) → the future script → the central theme. The same framework is a powerful self-reflection tool.
Slide 5: The most studied pattern? REDEMPTION — a scene that moves from bad to good. Suffering that led to growth. People who naturally tell redemptive stories report higher wellbeing & lower depression.
Slide 6: Its opposite is CONTAMINATION — a good scene spoiled by a bad ending. But careful: redemption isn't toxic positivity. The healthiest stories sit in the pain honestly first, THEN trace what grew.
Slide 7: Two engines of a good story: AGENCY (I'm an active force in my life) + COMMUNION (love & connection). Agency is the one most tied to mental health.
Slide 8: The stunning part: in people going through therapy, the story often got more AGENTIC *before* symptoms lifted. They started authoring instead of being the victim — and then the healing followed.
Slide 9: The best news: your story is EDITABLE. You can't change the events. You can change the meaning, the emphasis, and the through-line. Expressive writing (15 min, a few days) is one of the most studied tools.
Slide 10 (CTA): This week: name your chapters. Find the agency the hard times tried to erase. Write the next chapter you actually want to live. Full guide at bluemindbodyandsoul.com (link in bio). 📖

Caption

Ask someone who they are and, sooner or later, they tell you a story. Not a personality-test result — a story. Where they came from, the moment everything changed, where it's all heading. Psychologists call this NARRATIVE IDENTITY, and three decades of research say the kind of story you tell isn't just a side effect of how your life has gone. It quietly shapes how your life goes from here — your resilience, your mental health, your sense of meaning. A few of the findings I find most hopeful: 📖 The most studied pattern is REDEMPTION — scenes that move from bad to good. People who naturally tell redemptive stories tend to report higher wellbeing and lower depression. (Important: that's not toxic positivity. The healthiest version sits in the pain honestly first.) 📖 AGENCY — the sense that you're an active author, not a passive victim — has a striking link to mental health. In people going through therapy, the story often became more agentic BEFORE symptoms lifted. 📖 And the whole thing is editable. You can't change what happened. You can change the meaning you've assigned it. So here's your invitation: take an hour with a notebook this week and read your own book on purpose. Title the chapters. Find the agency the hard times tried to erase. Then write the next chapter you actually want to live. Full essay in the link in bio. — Josh (Educational, not a substitute for therapy.

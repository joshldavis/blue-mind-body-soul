10-Slide Carousel Script

Slide 1 (Hook): You open the app to kill 90 seconds. You close it 20 minutes later feeling worse about your body, your job, your life. Nothing bad happened. Here's what actually did. 📱
Slide 2: You're running 200,000-year-old software on a machine built to exploit it. In 1954, psychologist Leon Festinger showed humans have a built-in drive to measure ourselves against others. That instinct evolved for a village of ~150 people — not an infinite feed.
Slide 3: The trap: you're comparing your raw, unedited INSIDE to everyone else's highlight reel — filtered, cropped, posted on purpose. The math was never going to work in your favor.
Slide 4: What the research really says? It's debated — and honest. On average the effect is modest. BUT: cut-back experiments matter. Students who capped social apps at 30 min/day for 3 weeks reported LESS loneliness and depression.
Slide 5: "Screen time" is too broad to be useful — like calling sugar "food." What moves your mental health is 4 specific mechanisms. Name them and they lose their grip. 👇
Slide 6: Mechanism 1 — UPWARD COMPARISON. We compare toward people who have more. The feed makes the finish line move every time you refresh. You were happy with your kitchen until the renovation reel.
Slide 7: Mechanism 2 — FOMO. The anxious sense others are having rewarding experiences you're missing. Mechanism 3 — THE SLOT MACHINE. Unpredictable rewards (variable-ratio) are the hardest-to-quit pattern known to psychology. Pull-to-refresh = a lever.
Slide 8: Mechanism 4 — SLEEP THEFT. The most consistent finding in the whole literature. The feed pushes bedtime later AND keeps your brain pinging. Lose the sleep and you've degraded mood + focus before the day even starts.
Slide 9: Reclaim it (no quitting required): 1) Curate ruthlessly — mute anything that makes you feel smaller. 2) Add friction — kill notifications, off the home screen. 3) Run the 30-min experiment. 4) Protect the first + last hour. 5) Replace, don't just remove.
Slide 10 (CTA): Comparison isn't the enemy — UNCONSCIOUS comparison is. Notice the feeling, name the mechanism, choose what's next. Full guide on the blog (link in bio). Save this for the next time the scroll gets you. 💙

Main Post

Ever open an app to kill 90 seconds and close it 20 minutes later feeling quietly worse — about your body, your career, your last vacation? Nothing bad happened. So why the dip? It's social comparison, and it's one of the oldest instincts we have. In 1954, psychologist Leon Festinger showed we're wired to measure ourselves against the people around us. That worked beautifully in a world of 150 people you actually knew. It works against you when it's an infinite feed of strangers showing only their highlight reel. The honest science (I won't overstate it): on average the effect on mood is modest and genuinely debated. But the cut-back experiments are telling — when people limit their use, well-being tends to rise. In one study, capping social apps at 30 minutes a day for three weeks reduced loneliness and depression. You don't have to quit. You have to get conscious. Mute what makes you feel smaller. Kill the notifications. Cap your worst app at 30 minutes. And protect the first and last hour of your day, phone-free. Comparison isn't the enemy. Doing it unconsciously is. 👉 Question for you: what's the ONE account or app that most reliably leaves you feeling worse — and what would happen if you muted it for two weeks?

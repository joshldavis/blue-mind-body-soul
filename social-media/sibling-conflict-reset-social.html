10-Slide Carousel Script

Slide 1 (Hook): Your kids fight constantly. Here's the uncomfortable truth: the fight is doing a JOB for them. A 6-step reset. 👥
Slide 2: Stop asking "who started it?" Start asking "what keeps this going?" Sibling fights follow predictable loops — and loops can be redesigned.
Slide 3: STEP 1: 48-hour diagnostic. For every fight, note the TRIGGER, each child's BEHAVIOR, YOUR response, and the OUTCOME (who "won"?).
Slide 4: STEP 2: Find the why. Two common drivers: CONTROL & AVOIDANCE (a fight makes homework disappear) and JUSTICE & PROTEST ("That's not fair!").
Slide 5: The hidden cycle: one child escalates → the other reacts → parents intervene → attention flows to the conflict. Fighting gets rewarded for BOTH kids.
Slide 6: STEP 3: Trigger → Behavior → Reinforcer. Asked to clean up → snaps at sibling → avoids the task AND gets your attention. Make the outburst stop working.
Slide 7: STEP 4: Three house rules. 1) No hurting (body or words). 2) Don't interrupt others' activities. 3) Don't involve parents unless someone is unsafe.
Slide 8: Two-level response. LEVEL 1 bickering: ignore it — attention is fuel. LEVEL 2 aggression: immediate removal + cool-off. No debate in the heat of the moment.
Slide 9: STEP 5: A 2-week plan. Week 1: rules, tracking, routines. Week 2: daily 5-min role-plays, a shared reward (family movie points), less refereeing.
Slide 10 (CTA): STEP 6: Look beyond the behavior. Fighting can be a proxy for stress, school pressure, or unmet needs. Treat the system, not only the behavior. Full guide: link in bio 📌

Caption

If your house sounds like a referee convention every afternoon, this one's for you. 👥 Sibling fights feel random. They almost never are. They follow loops: a trigger, a behavior, and a payoff — often a payoff we accidentally provide (our full, undivided attention). The reset in 6 steps: 1️⃣ Run a 48-hour diagnostic (trigger, behavior, your response, outcome) 2️⃣ Find the why: control/avoidance or justice/protest 3️⃣ Map the Trigger → Behavior → Reinforcer loop 4️⃣ 3 house rules + a two-level response + replacement scripts + praise for cooperation 5️⃣ Commit to a 2-week plan 6️⃣ Look beyond the behavior — is the fighting carrying stress from somewhere else? Heads up: when attention stops flowing to fights, they often get briefly WORSE before they get better. That usually means it's working. Which step would help your family most?

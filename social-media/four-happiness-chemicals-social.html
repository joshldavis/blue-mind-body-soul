10-Slide Carousel Script

Slide 1 (Hook): That "hack your happy hormones" infographic on your feed? A psychologist's honest take: half right, importantly wrong. 🧠
Slide 2: The four chemicals are real: DOPAMINE, SEROTONIN, OXYTOCIN, ENDORPHINS. But your brain is not a vending machine — these are messengers, not moods. Each does dozens of jobs. There's no blood test for "happiness levels."
Slide 3: The frame that actually works: behaviors move systems → systems shape mood. Mood follows behavior far more faithfully than behavior follows mood. That's the whole game.
Slide 4: DOPAMINE isn't the pleasure chemical — it's the PURSUIT chemical. Berridge's lab showed it fires for wanting, not liking. It spikes in anticipation, drops when the chase ends. That's why buying the thing beats... no wait, why WANTING the thing beats buying it. 🎯
Slide 5: What moves dopamine well: visible progress on goals that matter. A checklist you physically cross off. A skill built stage by stage. What trains it badly: infinite feeds and ultra-processed food — big hits for zero effort make every slow reward feel flat.
Slide 6: SEROTONIN is your mood thermostat, and ~90% of it is made in your gut. The levers are almost embarrassingly unsexy: morning light (10-20 min outside), protein with real carbs, steady rhythmic movement. It doesn't respond to hacks. It responds to routines. ☀️
Slide 7: OXYTOCIN is the bonding signal — released by touch, voice, eye contact, feeding people, generosity, petting a dog. Fine print: it bonds you to YOUR people, not everyone. And a heart emoji barely moves it. A 10-minute phone call does. 🤝
Slide 8: ENDORPHINS = your body's own painkiller. Triggered by effort past comfortable, group laughter, singing together, sauna, cold water. (Honest correction: the "runner's high" is probably as much endocannabinoids as endorphins. The lever is the same: effort is the price of admission.) 🏃
Slide 9: Now the part the infographic hides: it's the SAME behaviors on all four lists. Movement hits all four. A shared meal hits at least three. You don't need four protocols. You need one integrated life: light, effort, real food, your people.
Slide 10 (CTA): One honest boundary: persistent low mood is not a hormone-hacking problem — clinical depression is real, treatable, and not your fault. Talk to a professional. Full breakdown + a 1-week experiment on the blog (link in bio). Save this. 💙

Caption

Your brain is not a vending machine. 🧠 The "happy hormones" infographic gets the molecules right — dopamine, serotonin, oxytocin, endorphins — and the frame wrong. These aren't four dials you turn with chocolate, sunlight, hugs, and jogging. They're messenger systems, and what the science actually supports is subtler and more hopeful: specific, repeatable behaviors reliably shift these systems, and mood follows behavior far more faithfully than behavior follows mood. The detail that changed how I think about it: dopamine isn't about pleasure — it's about pursuit. It fires for wanting, not liking. Which explains the scroll that never satisfies, and why crossing something off a physical checklist feels absurdly good. And here's the part the infographics leave out: look across all four systems and the same behaviors keep showing up. Movement touches all four. A shared meal touches at least three. Morning light, real food, laughter, purposeful work, your people. That's not four protocols. That's one integrated life, described from four chemical angles. I spent a decade in professional kitchens before psychology, and I keep coming back to this: cooking for people you love hits pursuit, connection, and warmth in a single act. Our species figured this out long before neuroscience named the molecules. ⚠️ Honest boundary: if your low mood is persistent, that's not a hormone-hacking problem. Clinical depression is real and treatable. Please talk to a professional. There's a 1-week experiment in the full post — link in bio. What's the one behavior on this list you've been skipping? 👇

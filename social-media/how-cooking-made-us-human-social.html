Slide 1 (Hook): The most important technology in human history isn't the wheel, the engine, or the chip. It's a fire — and a root pushed into the coals. 🔥
Slide 2: ~1 million years ago, deep inside Wonderwerk Cave in South Africa: ash and burned bone, 30 meters from the entrance. Too deep for a wildfire. Heated to campfire temperature. Someone BUILT that fire.
Slide 3: Here's the puzzle it solves. Humans are weird in two opposite directions: huge, expensive brains + small, dainty guts. A gorilla has the reverse. We traded the big gut for the big brain. How did we afford it?
Slide 4: Your brain is 2% of your body weight and burns ~20% of your energy. Every minute. Even asleep. It runs on ~12 watts and NEVER switches off. That organ needed a calorie surplus our ancestors didn't have. Until…
Slide 5: COOKING = digestion outside the body. Heat bursts plant cells, gelatinizes starch, unwinds proteins. Same food, far more calories, far less chewing. (Great apes chew up to HALF their waking hours. We got those hours back.)
Slide 6: That's Richard Wrangham's "cooking hypothesis": cooking paid for the human brain. Right on cue, ~1.8M years ago, Homo erectus shows up with a bigger brain and smaller jaws, teeth & gut. The body of an animal built around a campfire.

Slide 8: Fire also invented THE EVENING. Hours not for foraging or fleeing — but for sitting in a pool of light, talking, telling stories, reading faces. The campfire was the first classroom, first theater, first church.
Slide 9: The takeaway your nervous system already knows: we did not evolve to eat alone, standing at a counter, scrolling. Eating together is the oldest human ritual there is. The table is the fire now. 🍴

Main Post

A million years ago, deep inside a cave in South Africa, someone built a fire and cooked dinner. Archaeologists found the ashes — burned bone heated to exactly campfire temperature, thirty meters too deep for any wildfire to reach. That humble act may be the reason you have a human brain. Here's the short version: brains are metabolically ruinous (yours burns ~20% of your energy, around the clock), and raw food couldn't pay the bill — great apes spend up to half their waking hours just chewing. Cooking changed the math. Heat is digestion that happens outside the body: it unlocks calories, cuts chewing time, and let our ancestors trade the giant gut for the giant brain. Not long after cooking enters the story, Homo erectus shows up with exactly that anatomy. But as a psychologist (and a former professional cook), my favorite part isn't the metabolism. It's this: a root in the embers is a promise. Cooking made our species practice patience every single night. And fire invented the evening — the first hours humans ever spent simply sitting together, talking, telling stories. We did not evolve to eat alone, standing at a counter, scrolling. For two million years, the shared meal is what made us an "us." This week's post tells the whole story — the cave, the science, the honest scientific debates, and a "recipe" you can genuinely cook tonight, a million years later.

10-Slide Carousel Script

Slide 1 (Hook): No parent says “I just want a higher reading level.” We want kind, brave, honest kids. Here's the free, 15-minute tool that quietly builds exactly that. 📖
Slide 2: When a child hears a story, their brain doesn't file it as information — it runs a simulation. They mentally walk the character's path and try on their choices. A story is a flight simulator for the moral life.
Slide 3: The science: the more fiction kids read, the stronger their THEORY OF MIND — understanding that others think and feel differently than they do. (Raymond Mar's research.) Empathy is a trainable skill. Story is the gym.
Slide 4: Young kids don't learn morality from rules. They learn it from EMOTION + EXAMPLE — by loving a character and feeling what happens to them. That felt arc beats 100 reminders to “be good.”
Slide 5: The part parents miss: the magic isn't only the book — it's the CONVERSATION. Pause and ask: “Why did she do that?” “What would you have done?” Then be quiet and let them think. (It's called dialogic reading.)
Slide 6: Pair ONE virtue with ONE story. “Integrity” is a vapor. A little fox who owns up to a lie is unforgettable. Give the value a face and a plot.
Slide 7: Ages 4–7: Giraffes Can't Dance (perseverance) · Stand Tall, Molly Lou Melon (courage) · Ruthie and the Teeny Tiny Lie (honesty) · Bear Says Thanks (gratitude).
Slide 8: Ages 9–11: Because of Winn-Dixie (forgiveness) · The Hundred Dresses (standing up) · Stargirl (authenticity) · The Junkyard Wonders (difference as strength).
Slide 9: A story read aloud and an infinite feed do almost OPPOSITE things to a developing mind. One builds attention, empathy, patience. The other trains speed, switching, self-display. The read-aloud rebuilds what the feed erodes.

Caption

Ask any parent what they want for their kid and almost no one says “a higher reading level.” They say kind. Brave. Honest. Resilient. Here's the quietly stunning part: one of the most powerful character-building tools ever invented costs a library card and 15 minutes of your evening. It's a story, read aloud, in your lap. 📖 When kids hear a story, the brain runs a simulation — they try on the character's choices from the inside. Long before a child can define “courage,” they can FEEL what it's like to stand up like Molly Lou Melon or tell the truth like Ruthie. The research backs it up: the more fiction kids read (and TALK about), the better they get at understanding other people. Empathy is partly a trained skill — and story is the gym. The move most parents miss isn't picking the perfect book. It's the conversation around it: ✅ Read it like you mean it (emotion is the carrier signal) ✅ Stop once and wonder out loud: “What would you have done?” ✅ Name the virtue, lightly: “That took courage, didn't it?” ✅ Re-read the favorites — repetition is how it sinks in Pick one virtue your family is actually working on this season. Let a story do the teaching that nagging never could. Save this for bedtime. 📚 — Josh (Educational, not clinical advice — if you have concerns about your child's development, a pediatrician is the right call.)

Character, in other words.

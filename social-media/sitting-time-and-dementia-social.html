Caption

You can run six days a week, lift heavy, eat clean — and still spend so much of your day sitting that you're quietly aging your brain. That's the uncomfortable takeaway from a 2023 JAMA study that tracked 49,841 older adults using wrist accelerometers for a week, then followed them for nearly 7 years. The finding: sitting more than 10 hours a day was linked to sharply higher dementia risk. At 12 hours/day, risk was 63% higher. At 15 hours/day, more than 3x higher. And — this is the part that surprised everyone — the relationship held even after adjusting for vigorous exercise. Your morning workout doesn't buy you a free pass on the next 11 hours of sitting. Most Americans average 9.5 hours of sitting per day. Right at the inflection point. The good news: the action items are free. Walking pad. Walking meetings. Stand for phone calls. Post-dinner walks. Get up every 30 minutes. Cut one passive TV session per week and replace it with something on your feet. Sedentary time is the loudest, cheapest lever in long-term brain health. Make sitting the thing you do least. Full article + the science + a protocol in the link in bio. 🧠 — Josh

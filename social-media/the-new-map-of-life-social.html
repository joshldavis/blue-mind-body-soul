Main Post

Living to 100 may soon be ordinary. But are our lives designed for it? The Stanford Center on Longevity's "New Map of Life" report notes that life expectancy doubled between 1900 and 2000 — yet healthspan (the years we're healthy and capable) hasn't kept pace with lifespan. Their big idea: longevity isn't just a medical project, it's a design project for how we learn, work, rest, save, and connect. On the blog this week I break down the report's key ideas and five practical moves you can start today — from building an environment that makes healthy choices easy to treating life transitions as chapters rather than failures. Discussion question: What's one thing you want to still be able to do at 85 — and what's one small step you could take this week to protect it?

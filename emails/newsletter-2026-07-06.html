The friendly rot: fermentation & your gut

Hey friend,

Here’s one of the most counterintuitive ideas in all of human food: the way to keep food from going bad is to let it go bad — but in exactly the right way, on purpose. That’s fermentation. You invite the good microbes in, they produce acids and alcohols that crowd out the dangerous ones, and along the way they hand you sauerkraut, kimchi, yogurt, kefir, miso, and sourdough.

What amazes me is that humans mastered this for thousands of years without knowing microbes even existed — guiding invisible organisms by intuition and tradition long before Pasteur explained why any of it worked. And now science has caught up with a humbling twist: the same foods our ancestors made for preservation turn out to nourish the trillions of microbes in our gut — the ones that shape our digestion, our immunity, and even our mood.

This week’s takeaway: Not everything sour is alive. Most supermarket pickles and kraut are made with vinegar and then pasteurized, which kills the very cultures that make fermented food valuable. If it’s shelf-stable, it’s basically dead. Look for “live” or “raw” in the refrigerated section — or make your own, where the one rule that matters is: keep everything under the brine.

What actually moves your four “happiness chemicals”

Hey friend,

You’ve seen the infographic: dopamine = chocolate, serotonin = sunshine, oxytocin = hugs, endorphins = jogging. Collect all four, unlock happiness. This week I finally wrote down what I actually think about it as a psychologist — because it’s half right, and wrong in a way that quietly sets people up to feel broken.

The chemicals are real. But they’re messengers, not moods — there’s no dial to turn and no blood test for “happiness levels.” The biggest myth in the set: dopamine isn’t the pleasure chemical. It’s the pursuit chemical. It fires for wanting, not liking — which explains, in one stroke, why the scroll never satisfies and why crossing something off a paper checklist feels absurdly good.

This week’s takeaway: Look across all four systems and the same short list of behaviors keeps appearing. Movement touches all four. A shared meal touches at least three. Morning light, real food, hard and meaningful work, laughter, your people. It was never four protocols to optimize — it’s one integrated life, described from four chemical angles. Mood follows behavior far more faithfully than behavior follows mood.

The full post walks through each system honestly — including the fine print the infographics skip (oxytocin bonds you to your people, the “runner’s high” may owe as much to endocannabinoids, and persistent low mood is a professional-help problem, not a hacking problem). It ends with a one-week experiment: daily morning light, one checklist-driven block of meaningful work, three hard workouts, two shared home-cooked meals. Then one question: do you feel more like yourself?

Not because an app is magic — because a fixed anchor beats willpower, and the serotonin system rewards routines, not intentions.

One small experiment for the week: pick the lever you most neglect — morning light, hard effort, real food, or actual time with your people — and schedule it like an appointment, three times. Not as a treat. As infrastructure.

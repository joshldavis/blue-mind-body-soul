The sugar trap: it was never about willpower

Hey friend,

Here’s something I’d like to take off your shoulders this week. That 9pm reach for something sweet you didn’t really decide to want? It’s not a character flaw. It’s a two-million-year-old appetite meeting a world it was never built for.

For almost all of human history, sweetness was rare and precious — ripe fruit, wild honey, safe calories in a world where calories weren’t guaranteed. So evolution gave us a powerful craving for sweet and never bothered with an off switch. It didn’t need to: the scarcity was the off switch. Then we learned to make sugar cheaply and endlessly, and engineered it into nearly everything. The accelerator is ancient. The brake belonged to the world — and we removed it.

This week’s takeaway: The struggle with sugar isn’t weakness — it’s mismatch. And the fix isn’t guilt or quitting forever (that backfires). It’s restoring rarity: make the sweet thing occasional, real, and worth savoring. Less sweet, better made, fully tasted — the pleasure is greater, not lesser, for it.

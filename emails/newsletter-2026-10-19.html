The trait that predicts your health, your relationships, and your lifespan

Hey friend,

I want to tell you about a research finding that reorganized how I think about personality. Evolutionary psychologists have spent 40 years measuring something called life history strategy — and it sorts everyone somewhere on a spectrum from “fast” to “slow.”

Fast strategy: more partners, earlier risk-taking, live for right now, less investment in any one relationship. Slow strategy: fewer, deeper relationships, delayed gratification, heavy investment in a smaller number of long-term outcomes — including your own future health.

The key takeaway: A 2014 meta-analysis pooling thousands of participants found that a “slow” score correlates with higher conscientiousness, stronger self-control, more relationship stability, lower substance involvement, and — the one that stopped me — better self-reported physical and mental functioning on a standard medical health survey. It's roughly 65% heritable. The other 35% comes from how predictable and safe your environment is, which means you still have real leverage over it, whatever your childhood looked like.

In the full piece I lay out five specific ways to shift toward a slow strategy — starting with manufacturing predictability in your own life and practicing delayed gratification somewhere genuinely low-stakes.

On the practice side: a short daily mindfulness session measurably improves your ability to sit with an urge without acting on it — which is the entire mechanism behind delayed gratification. Headspace has structured programs built around exactly this.

The richest people own their time.

Hey friend,

Tim Ferriss tells a story about a man he met on a flight out of Las Vegas — casinos, a diamond ring, half a million dollars on Vegas trips, and, in his telling, completely dead inside. He’d hated nearly every job that built the fortune. He’d traded thirty years of his one life for the right to look successful in his sixties. Ferriss has a blunt word for living that way: being a deferrer. Work to the bone now, bank the money, finally start living somewhere off in the haze of retirement.

This week’s essay is about the antidote — what researchers call time affluence. And the science is quietly stunning: people who prioritize time over money report greater happiness even after you account for how much money they actually have. Spending to outsource a task you dread beats buying more stuff. The one caveat? Free time is a hill, not a ladder — too little is “time poverty,” but a flood of empty hours costs you meaning. The win is enough time, spent on purpose.

This week’s takeaway: Time is the only currency you can’t earn back, so weigh your choices in time, not just dollars. Run a quick 80/20 audit — which 20% of your week gives you most of your energy and joy, and which 20% causes most of your stress? Protect the first, shrink the second. Then use Parkinson’s Law on purpose: give one task half the time you normally would and watch it still get done.

The full essay walks through the whole toolkit — the deferred-life trap, the 80/20 of a whole life, the information diet, and the case for mini-retirements instead of hoarding all your freedom for the end.

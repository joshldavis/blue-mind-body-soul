Win the first hour of your day.

Hey friend,

We tend to hunt for the one big lever — the supplement, the cold plunge, the gadget — when the real lever is the shape of an ordinary day. This week’s essay is a science-backed daily blueprint built around your circadian rhythm: the roughly 24-hour clock that quietly governs your energy, mood, focus, and the depth of your sleep.

The whole thing comes down to answering one question clearly, all day long — is it day or night? — with light, movement, food, and caffeine. Three moves carry most of the weight: get outdoor light in the first hour after waking (even on a cloudy day it’s ~10,000 lux versus ~500 indoors), delay your caffeine 90 to 120 minutes so it isn’t sitting on top of unfinished grogginess, and hold a fixed wake time all seven days. That last one is the real reason Mondays feel brutal — sleeping in on the weekend shifts your internal clock later.

This week’s takeaway: Consistency beats perfection. You don’t need the whole routine by Monday — aim for about 85% of days, and start with just three habits: morning light, delayed caffeine, and a wake time you actually keep. Most of the other benefits ride on the back of a well-set clock.

The full essay walks through the whole day — the breathing tool that switches on alertness in minutes, NSDR for an afternoon recharge, what to eat at dinner for easier sleep, and the evening wind-down that makes recovery happen overnight.

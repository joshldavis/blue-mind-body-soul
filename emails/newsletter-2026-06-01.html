You’re the author, not just the character.

Hey friend,

Ask someone who they really are and, sooner or later, they don’t recite a list of traits — they tell you a story. Where they came from, the moment everything changed, the hard stretch they’re proud of surviving, where it’s all heading. Psychologists call this narrative identity, and three decades of research say the kind of story you tell isn’t just a byproduct of how your life has gone. It quietly shapes how your life goes from here — your resilience, your mood, your sense of meaning.

This week’s essay digs into the science. The most studied pattern is redemption — scenes that move from bad to good — which tracks with higher wellbeing (though, importantly, it’s not the same as toxic positivity). And the finding that stopped me cold: in people going through therapy, their story often became more agentic — author instead of victim — before their symptoms lifted. The story moved first. The healing followed.

This week’s takeaway: The events of your past are fixed, but the story you build from them is editable — and that’s where a surprising amount of your wellbeing lives. Take an hour with a notebook this week: name the chapters of your life, then re-read one hard chapter and find the agency the “victim” version quietly erased. You’re not rewriting the facts. You’re widening the frame.

The essay walks through the full toolkit — the Life Story Interview framework, expressive writing, and how to draft a future chapter you actually want to live — plus an honest note on when this work is better done with a professional alongside you.

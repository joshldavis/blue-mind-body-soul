A longer life needs a better map

Hey friend,

Here's a number that reframed my week: according to the Stanford Center on Longevity, human life expectancy doubled between 1900 and 2000 — more gain in a single century than in all prior human history combined. By mid-century, living to 100 is expected to be commonplace.

The catch is that our institutions were built for lives half as long. School, then work, then a retirement cliff. That script no longer fits the years many of us are likely to have.

The key takeaway: Longevity isn't only a medical project — it's a design project. The goal isn't more years, it's more healthspan: years you're healthy, capable, connected, and engaged. And the people who thrive will treat life transitions as chapters to plan for, not failures to survive.

In the full piece I walk through Stanford's New Map of Life and five practical moves — from building an environment that makes healthy choices easy, to keeping your brain learning at every age.
